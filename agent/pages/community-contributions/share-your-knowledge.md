# Share Your Knowledge, No Technical Skills Needed

You do not need to know how to code to help. If you use assistive technology, care for someone
who does, or have found something that works, your experience is useful to other people.

You write in your own words. A reviewer does the technical part.

## What you can share

- A tool or app that helps you
- A tip or a way of doing something
- Your own experience
- A useful link
- A correction to a page on this site

## How to share

1. Open the [sharing form](https://github.com/alexarguello/accessibility-hub/issues/new?template=share-content.yml).
2. Sign in to GitHub, or create a free account. This is the only account you need.
3. Fill in the form. Only three answers are required: what you are sharing, a short title, and
   your text.
4. Tick the two boxes at the end and send the form.

You can write in English or in Spanish.

## What happens after you send it

1. **A reviewer reads it.** They may ask you a question on the same page where you sent the form.
2. **The reviewer checks it.** They check facts and links, and remove anything that could be
   medical, legal or financial advice.
3. **The reviewer turns it into a page.** They choose where it belongs on the site, add the
   technical details the site needs, and tidy the formatting. Your meaning stays the same.
4. **You check it.** You get a link to the draft page. Say if anything is wrong.
5. **It is published.** Your name appears as the author, unless you asked to stay anonymous.

## Things to know

- **Everything you write in the form is public.** Do not share private details about yourself or
  about another person.
- **Your text may be edited** so it is clear and easy to read.
- **Your text is published under the site's documentation licence**, CC BY 4.0. Other people may
  reuse it if they give credit.
- **You need a GitHub account** to send the form. If that is a barrier for you, ask someone you
  trust to send it for you and to say that it is yours.

## For reviewers

When a form arrives:

1. Reply within a week, even if only to say you have seen it.
2. Check every fact that can be checked, and every link. Ask the contributor when unsure.
3. Create the page from the page template, fill in the frontmatter, and put the contributor in
   `author` the way they asked to be credited.
4. Keep the contributor's own words where you can. Edit for plain language, not for style.
5. Open a pull request that mentions the form's issue number, and post the preview link on the issue.
6. Merge after the contributor agrees, or after two weeks without a reply.

If you already know Git and Markdown, see [Contributing to the Accessibility Hub](https://alexarguello.github.io/accessibility-hub/docs/community-contributions/contributing/).
